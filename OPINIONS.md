# Dmitri's Opinions

> This is a compact, living map of durable beliefs, taste, values, critiques, and recurring judgments.
> It is not a knowledge base, a task list, or a record of every technical detail.
> The initial entries are an agent-generated synthesis of explicit principles in Dmitri's existing local instructions and documents and should be reviewed for accuracy.
> Reorganize the sections when a clearer structure emerges instead of preserving them by inertia.

## Information and knowledge

- Durable knowledge should be local-first, plain text, tool-agnostic, and legible years from now.
- Every durable fact should have one authoritative owner, because duplicate truth creates drift.
- Current truth is more useful than an append-only history that leaves contradictions unresolved.
- Good capture preserves meaning, uncertainty, dates, links, attribution, and voice instead of laundering them into false certainty.
- Organization should improve retrieval and reuse, not become a deep taxonomy maintained for its own sake.
- A focused synthesis with evidence and source links is more useful than an unstructured collection of copied material.

## Agents and automation

- Agents do better work when they can find the small amount of context that actually shapes a decision.
- Agent instructions should point to authoritative files instead of repeating information that the repository already makes clear.
- Automation should reduce repeated work without hiding evidence, uncertainty, or the boundary between source material and interpretation.
- Weak signals should not become durable beliefs merely because an agent can summarize them.
- An agent should surface contradictions and stale assumptions rather than averaging them into a smooth but inaccurate story.

## AI, learning, and responsibility

- I paid a massive personal price to leave my job, move back in with my parents, and build my business, and that sacrifice led me to hard-earned realizations about how AI should and should not be used.
- AI should be used most for personal learning and for applying what has been learned.
- The more work AI does, the more work there is to review, because generated output does not remove the responsibility to understand it.
- If I do not understand what AI created, I cannot review it meaningfully, which means I do not really know what I created.
- This matters especially when an output can influence large sums of money or significant investments of time.
- When the stakes are low, gambling on output I do not fully understand can be a reasonable choice.

## Engineering and product

- Long-term quality, simplicity, robustness, and maintainability matter more than minimizing development cost.
- Clever automation and premature abstraction are liabilities when they obscure ownership or make the system harder to understand.
- An abstraction should be earned by evidence from more than one real use case rather than extracted speculatively.
- Proof should match the claim, and strong safety or lifecycle claims require explicit, value-safe evidence.
- The common path should be fast and low-strain, while the final proof path should be strong enough to catch serious failures.
- Real operating constraints and measurable value should lead product decisions, rather than technology being treated as the value case by itself.
- Technical implementation details matter mainly as evidence of broader engineering values, tradeoffs, and constraints.

## Truth, privacy, and risk

- Privacy is an operational boundary, not a cosmetic preference.
- Sensitive material should be isolated deliberately, and credentials should never be placed in ordinary documentation.
- Professional claims should be bounded by verifiable evidence rather than made more impressive through implication.
- A system should preserve uncertainty when the evidence is incomplete instead of guessing in order to appear helpful.
- Validation, lint, test quality, and flakiness are part of engineering quality rather than optional polish.

## Change and decisions

- Small pilots and real usage should inform permanent workflow and synchronization choices.
- Durable decisions should record their rationale and the evidence that would cause them to be reconsidered.
- When new evidence changes a view, revise the current owner and preserve the meaningful history without allowing contradictory truth to accumulate forever.
- A good system makes the safe and truthful path easy enough that it is followed during ordinary work.

## Review prompts

- Which entries are genuinely mine rather than common consensus or an instruction I copied?
- Which entries are too strong, too vague, stale, or not falsifiable?
- Which entries are values, which are empirical beliefs, and which are predictions?
- Which new evidence refines, contradicts, or strengthens an existing entry?
